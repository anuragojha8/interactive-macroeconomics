Why saving more doesn’t make you richer today

Every economy faces the same trade. Consume now, or build something that produces later. The Solow model is what happens when you write that trade down as one line of state and let it run.

P1 stocks & flows P2 identities P4 assumptions

Chapter 2 left you with an economy that has no memory. Capital sat at whatever you set it to, forever, because nothing in the model could change it. That is why we wrote it K̄ — the overbar meant fixed.

Now we add one relation. Some of this year’s output gets built into next year’s capital, and some of the existing capital wears out. The bar comes off.

accumulation · P1 capital-accumulation
Kt+1  =  Kt  +  ( It − d̄Kt ) · Δt
$ + ($/year − $/year) × year = $ ✓

Drop the Δt and you are adding a stock to a flow. The engine refuses to run — and that refusal is the lesson.

show the code show the derivation

Two lines of arithmetic, and the model now has a past. Before you move anything, commit to an answer: if the country decides to invest 30% of output instead of 20%, what happens to output this year?

Predict before you run

Three sign predictions, then the model runs. Your calling record is kept — you will find out quickly where your intuition is reliable and where it is not.

Most people say output rises. It does not. Not this year, not by a cent. Output depends on the capital that already exists, and no decision made today can retroactively build it. What moves immediately is the split: investment up 50%, consumption down 12.5%. Output only responds from next year, through the capital that this year’s investment actually creates.

Break it

Set d̄ = 0 so capital never wears out. The steady state disappears, the solver reports that it never settles, and the feedback loop crosses a gain of 1 into amplifying. Two independent parts of the engine agree, which is the point.
